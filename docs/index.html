ML-Guided Demonstration Selection for Interpretable Few-Shot Alzheimer’s Disease Classification with LLMs

Lightweight machine learning guides in-context example selection for LLM-based classification and natural-language reasoning from MRI-derived features.

Thisara Kumarasiri · Pubudu Sanjeewani · Azadeh Alavi · Estrid He · Tabinda Sarwar

School of Computing Technologies, RMIT University, Melbourne, Victoria 3000, Australia

Code Dataset Accuracy Interpretability Citation

Abstract

Machine-learning (ML) models can effectively classify Alzheimer’s disease (AD) using MRI-derived structural features, but they provide limited explanation of how multiple anatomical measurements contribute to individual predictions. Large language models (LLMs) can complement these models by generating both predictions and natural-language reasoning from structured neuroimaging measurements. However, the effectiveness of few-shot in-context learning depends strongly on the examples selected for each query.

This study proposes an ML-guided demonstration-selection strategy that uses discriminative information learned from labeled data to identify informative in-context examples. The approach combines global feature importance from logistic regression with gain-based information from XGBoost and is evaluated against zero-shot, random, similarity-based, and model-aware selection strategies on the ADNI dataset.

Using GPT-5.4 and Grok-4.2, the proposed method achieved the highest classification accuracy. Analysis of the generated reasoning also showed broader coverage of biologically relevant brain regions and generally appropriate directional interpretations of anatomical changes associated with Alzheimer’s disease.

Dataset and Feature Extraction

T1-weighted structural MRI scans from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) were processed with FreeSurfer to derive regional cortical and subcortical measurements used as structured inputs for the machine-learning models and LLMs.
